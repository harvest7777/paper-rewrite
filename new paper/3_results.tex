\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Noise floor}
\label{sec:floor}

Because command order rotates, the baseline is measured once in each position.
Table~\ref{tab:floor} reports how far apart its three means land --- the
apparent speedup this protocol produces when nothing has changed.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Apparent speedup measured between the baseline and itself, per graph
and per phase. \textbf{Each cell:} average the baseline's 10 timed runs within
each of the three command orderings, giving three means; form all three pairwise
ratios, larger divided by smaller; the cell is the largest of the three.}
\label{tab:floor}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
\textbf{Measurement} & \textbf{sparse-deep} & \textbf{default} & \textbf{dense-shallow} \\
\midrule
End-to-end            & 1.006$\times$ & 1.019$\times$ & 1.006$\times$ \\
\midrule
Initialization        & 1.018$\times$ & 1.119$\times$ & 1.060$\times$ \\
Ranking               & 1.020$\times$ & 1.015$\times$ & 1.003$\times$ \\
Crossing minimization & 1.010$\times$ & 1.015$\times$ & 1.006$\times$ \\
Coordinate assignment & 1.019$\times$ & 1.012$\times$ & 1.012$\times$ \\
Spline routing        & 1.010$\times$ & 1.007$\times$ & 1.005$\times$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Worked example, crossing minimization on sparse-deep: the three per-ordering
means are $61.47$, $62.07$ and $61.60$\,ms, giving pairwise ratios $1.010$,
$1.002$ and $1.008$. The largest is the cell.

The floor is not uniform, which is why we report it per graph and per phase.
Initialization on default is the noisiest cell at $1.119\times$ --- six times
the crossing-minimization floor on the same graph --- because it is parsing and
setup, sensitive to page cache. Pooling would let that one cell set a threshold
everywhere.

Three ratios per cell is a small sample, so each is a rough bound. That is the
cost of not conflating graphs.

\textbf{Throughout the rest of this section, we treat a measured ratio as a
change only if it exceeds the floor for that same phase and that same graph.}
Ratios that do not are marked in the tables that follow.

This floor is measured inside the same \texttt{hyperfine} invocations as the
results it bounds, on the same graphs, against the same competing commands. The
more conventional construction --- benchmarking separately-built identical
binaries --- places them in an invocation of their own, with a different command
set and thermal profile, and so describes a different experiment from the one
being guarded.

\subsection{Phase weights depend on shape, not size}

minimization takes $11.5\%$ of layout time on one shape and $64.0\%$ on
another, while coordinate assignment moves the other way, $42.1\%$ down to
$9.4\%$. This sets a different ceiling on each workload, and it is why one
optimization gives different end-to-end results on different graphs.

Twelve of the 30 entries exceed their floor: Claude on ranking, crossing

smallest survivor is $1.25\times$ against a $1.012\times$ floor, the largest

So Claude improves three phases: ranking by roughly $2\times$ everywhere,
crossing minimization by $1.47$ to $2.34\times$, coordinate assignment by $1.25$
to $1.62\times$. Codex improves crossing minimization by $1.31$ to $1.61\times$
and nothing else.

That is what produces its $0.99\times$ on sparse-deep. The optimization is real
at $1.31\times$ on the phase, but the phase is $11.5\%$ of that workload.

Neither agent touches initialization or spline routing, as expected: both worked
in \texttt{mincross.c}, and Claude also in \texttt{ns.c}.

\subsection{Correctness}

Both binaries produce \texttt{dot -Tdot} output byte-identical to the baseline
on every evaluation graph, with instrumentation both enabled and disabled.

We also ran the 260 graphs in GraphViz's own test suite through both binaries
and compared against the baseline byte for byte. Both agents changed
\texttt{mincross.c}, which only \texttt{dot} uses; Claude also changed
\texttt{ns.c}, which \texttt{neato} reaches as well, so we checked both
engines. All 1{,}040 comparisons are identical, with matching exit codes.

Reading the diffs against the prompt's disallowed shortcuts found no reduced
iteration counts, early exits, input special-casing or modified tests, and no
concurrency of any kind.
