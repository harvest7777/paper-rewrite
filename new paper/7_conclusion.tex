\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Eight agent runs, given identical checkouts of GraphViz and prompts naming no
target, all located the same expensive code. Finding the bottleneck was
evidently not the difficult part of the task.

What separated them was breadth. Every Claude run carried on into network
simplex, which serves two further phases, and improves three of five on every
shape we tested --- $1.39\times$ to $2.91\times$ end-to-end. Codex's one
shipping run optimized the phase it identified first and measures $1.03\times$
to $1.33\times$; on the sparsest shape that optimization works, at $1.32\times$
on its phase, and still yields nothing end-to-end because the phase is $12.4\%$
of the workload.

Codex's other three runs shipped nothing, each after building a change,
measuring it, and judging it not worth keeping. We take that at face value. It
does mean a two-run study would have described whichever run it happened to
draw, which is the strongest argument in this paper for repeating agent runs
before characterizing an agent.

Asking for concurrency changed what the agents built but not uniformly what it
bought. Two Claude runs under that prompt land close together end-to-end while
only one of them owes its result to threads, and the same prompt moved Codex to
prototype concurrency three times and ship it zero times.

The ceiling an optimization can reach is a property of the input, not of the
program. At fixed node count, crossing minimization ranges from $12.4\%$ to
$64.8\%$ of layout time across our three shapes, and on the sparsest one every
Claude run exceeds what any crossing-minimization optimization could achieve. A
single benchmark graph cannot show this.

The results are modest and we have tried to report them at the size they are.
The protocol may be the more durable contribution: balance command ordering and
take the floor from the baseline's own spread across those orderings; keep the
evaluation harness out of the agents' reach; forbid the shortcuts that
manufacture speedups; measure phases directly rather than by subtraction;
re-measure threaded binaries at one thread before calling a result parallel.
Each caught something a simpler design would have reported as a finding.

Immediate future work is to extend to a second graph size and to non-synthetic
inputs, to run the concurrent code under a dynamic race detector on a platform
where one works, and to complete the correctness checks named in
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.
